About The U.S. Census

The U.S. Census is a comprehensive decennial count and ongoing annual survey (American Community Survey) conducted by the federal government to measure the nation's population, demographic shifts, economic vitality, and social composition. For advertising agencies and brand clients, this data is an essential strategic foundation to ensure media investments resonate authentically with America's rapidly evolving consumer base.

Demographic Glossary of Terms (U.S. Census Bureau Definitions)

White (Non-Hispanic White) Non-Hispanic White, or NHW, refers to individuals claiming origin from Europe, the Middle East, or North Africa, who do not identify as Hispanic or Latino.
Black (Black or African American Only) Individuals having origins in any of the Black racial groups of Africa, excluding multiracial combinations.
Hispanic (Hispanic or Latino) Persons of Cuban, Mexican, Puerto Rican, South or Central American, or other Spanish culture/origin, regardless of race.
Asian (Asian/AAPI Only) Individuals having origins in the Far East, Southeast Asia, or the Indian subcontinent, as well as Pacific Islands.
Native American (AIAN) American Indian and Alaska Native persons having origins in any of the original peoples of North, Central, and South America.
Two or More Races (2+ Races) Individuals who self-identify with two or more distinct racial group classifications on Census surveys.
LGBTQ+ Self-reported sexual orientation and gender identity data synthesized from Gallup telephone surveys and demographic research partners.
