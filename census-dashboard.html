About The U.S. Census

The U.S. Census is a comprehensive decennial count and ongoing annual survey (American Community Survey) conducted by the federal government to measure the nation's population, demographic shifts, economic vitality, and social composition. For advertising agencies and brand clients, this data is an essential strategic foundation. It illuminates exactly who audiences are, identifies emerging demographic growth hubs, reveals cultural shifts, and details target market composition. Leveraging this intelligence ensures media investments, messaging, and creative strategy resonate authentically with America's rapidly evolving consumer base.

Demographic Glossary of Terms (U.S. Census Bureau Definitions)

White Tab (White, Non-Hispanic) Individuals claiming origin from Europe, the Middle East, or North Africa, who do not identify as Hispanic or Latino.
Black Tab (Black or African American Only) Individuals having origins in any of the Black racial groups of Africa, excluding multiracial combinations.
Hispanic Tab (Hispanic or Latino) Persons of Cuban, Mexican, Puerto Rican, South or Central American, or other Spanish culture/origin, regardless of race.
Asian Tab (Asian/AAPI Only) Individuals having origins in the Far East, Southeast Asia, or the Indian subcontinent, as well as Pacific Islands.
Native American (AIAN) American Indian and Alaska Native persons having origins in any of the original peoples of North, Central, and South America.
Two or More Races (2+ Races) Individuals who self-identify with two or more distinct racial group classifications on Census surveys.
LGBTQ+ Tab Self-reported sexual orientation and gender identity data synthesized from Gallup, The Trevor Project, GLAAD, and Williams Institute.
